DownloadHarvester is a software platform with three distinct products that all feed into each other. How It Works

The bloatware middleman — end to end

Step 1
Freeware dev packages their software
Using the official DownloadHarvester packaging method. One-time setup. Works with any software, no matter what it is.
→
Step 2
Download happens
Someone downloads the freeware. The DownloadHarvester sensor detects the download automatically.
→
Step 3
Bloatware company pays
Bloatware companies pay for the service. They want the downloads, the reach, the exposure.
→
Step 4
Dev gets paid
The freeware dev is paid automatically per download. No action. No work. No contract they have to manage. Sensors handle it all.

The freeware ad network works the same way in reverse — developers join the network, sponsors plug in, and ads run across the software. 10,000 developers in the network means hundreds of sponsors can reach them.

The itch.io desktop port is a separate product that runs alongside — it re-categorizes and filters games, and it can carry ads or bloatware from the network too. It's the distribution layer.

Why This Exists

For freeware developers

Freeware devs currently have two bad options: give their software away for nothing, or bundle bloatware themselves and deal with the contracts directly. DownloadHarvester gives them a third option — package it once with the official method, and get paid per download automatically. No bloatware contracts. No work. Just sensors that pay you when people download your software.

For bloatware companies

Bloatware companies want downloads. They want reach. They want exposure. DownloadHarvester gives them a clean way to pay for that — through the middleman — without having to contract with every freeware dev individually. They pay the service, the service pays the devs per download, everyone wins.
