The idea

Makeup for men. But dry. Invisible. And good for your skin.

Men’s grooming has two extremes: nothing or full-on cosmetics that feel feminine, transfer onto your collar, and leave your skin worse than before. MARKUP is the third option — products that do the job of makeup without feeling like makeup. Dry. Nearly invisible. Built to be comfortable enough that you forget you’re wearing them. And formulated so they’re not sabotaging your skin or hair while they do it.

Why a gel, not a spray or a powder

Sprays wash out or feel wet. Powders look dusty and feel like you’re wearing something obvious. A gel lets the color sit in the hair, not on top of it, and dry down to something that behaves like your hair just looks better. It’s the form factor that gets closest to “your hair, but thicker” without tipping anyone off.

The looksmaxxing angle, stated plainly

You’re here because you want to look better. Thicker hair is one of the highest-impact changes a man can make, and if you’re dealing with thinning or bald spots, this is the fastest way to fix the look today while you work on the longer-term stuff. No shame in that — the point is the result.

How it works

Three steps. No one notices.

The product is built to disappear into your routine. Apply it like any other styling product, let it dry, and go. The whole point is that you’re not wearing something obvious — your hair just looks better.

Step 01

Apply to thinning areas

Work the gel into the areas you want to fill in — part lines, crown, spots where hair is sparse. Like styling gel, but targeted where you need it.

Step 02

Let it dry

It sets dry on its own. No heat, no waiting around. Once it’s dry, the color is where it needs to be and the gel base is gone — you’re left with the visual effect.

Step 03

Walk out looking denser

Your hair looks thicker and more even. The bald spots and thin areas are filled in. Nothing rubs off, nothing feels weird, and no one’s the wiser.

The approach

Makeup, but actually healthy. And masculine.

Most “men’s makeup” products are just rebranded cosmetics that happen to come in a masculine package. MARKUP is built from a different premise: if a man is going to use a cosmetic product, it should not feel like a cosmetic, it should not damage his skin or hair, and it should look like he just has better hair — not like he’s wearing something.

Dry, not wet

The gel dries down completely. No wet look, no shine, no “I can tell you’re wearing product.” Once it sets, it’s dry — like your hair just happens to look better.

Doesn’t rub off

Transfer is the giveaway that kills the whole effect. MARKUP products are built to resist it — on your hands, your collar, your pillow. If it rubs off, it fails the job.

Good for your skin and hair

This is the real differentiator. A product you put on your face and scalp every day should not be sabotaging your skin or hair. The formulation angle is real, not marketing fluff — that’s the whole thesis of MARKUP.

Comfort is the point

If you can feel it, something’s wrong. The target is “you forget it’s there” — comfortable enough to wear all day without thinking about it. That’s the bar.

How it starts
MARKUP is drop-shipped first. The products come from existing supply chains and get slapped with the MARKUP brand and the clean pitch. Real product, real branding, real-looking page — but not yet our own manufacturing. This is the fastest way to get a real product in front of real customers and start learning what actually sells.
Where it goes
Then it becomes real. Once there’s demand and a real sales pitch that works, the goal is to move from drop-shipped to our own formulation and hardware — own the product chain. That’s the ALLSEER playbook: start with something that looks and sells like a real company, then build the real thing underneath it.
